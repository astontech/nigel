# The fact sheet

The material under the script. Every row is something an interviewer will pull on. A row is **filled** when it holds a specific fact the candidate stated; it is a **slot** when they didn't know, didn't say, or answered in generalities. Slots are written into the talk track as dashed placeholders and listed under *Before you rehearse*. The skill never invents a fact to fill a row.

| Row | Filled means | Asked in |
|---|---|---|
| What the system is for | One sentence a non-engineer would follow: what it records or does, for whom, at what scale. | Material |
| Why it was prioritized | The business reason the work happened: what was slow, unsafe, or costly; who was hurting. | Step 2 |
| Who the customer is | The teams or people who consume what the system produces, and what they do with it. | Step 2 |
| Timeline | When they joined, the month each piece shipped, what is in production today. | Step 2 |
| Team and your slice | Headcount by role, who is internal versus contract, where the candidate sits. | Step 2 |
| Exactly what you built | Per component: alone or shared, which parts. This row decides the "I" / "we" boundary. | Step 2 |
| What you didn't build | Named components they can describe but did not write, with a one-line definition each. | Step 2 |
| Scale | Volumes, counts, sizes: events per day, records, sites, users. | Step 2 |
| Alternatives rejected | At least two design choices with the alternative and the reason. Reasoning they can defend, even for choices made by others. | Step 3 |
| Who pushed back | A real disagreement, and the other side's best argument stated as its author would. | Step 3 |
| How you validated it | How they knew the output was right: tests, a shadow period, a consumer's reaction, measured numbers. | Step 3 |
| What broke | A production problem with their name on it: what it looked like from outside, how they found it, how long it took. | Step 4 |
| What it cost | A real cost: time lost, a missed alternative, a relationship, money. | Step 4 |
| What you'd change | Something specific. "Nothing" is a slot. | Step 4 |
| How the engagement is going | For a client interview: how the last engagement ended or the current one is going, whether they would be taken back. | Step 4 |

## Judging an answer

- Specific and traceable to their words → filled. Write it in their words, tightened.
- "Don't know", "not sure", "I'd have to check" → slot. Say nothing about it; the drill is where it gets probed.
- A generality ("we made sure it was reliable") → slot, with the generality kept as the prompt text so they see what to replace.
- A number they hedge ("maybe a million?") → filled with the hedge preserved ("around a million, confirm").
